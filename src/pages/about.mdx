*For a longer version including trivia and pics, see [Even more about me](/more-about).*

I am originally from Trieste, Italy, but I spent the last 10 years hopping around the world for my studies. I graduated from an undergrad CS program in Québec, Canada, before returning to my hometown for an MSc in Data Science at the [University of Trieste](https://portale.units.it/en) and [SISSA](https://www.sissa.it/). My master's coincided with the first pre-trained Transformers (BERT, GPT-2), so I started working in NLP relatively early — first as a research assistant in the [ItaliaNLP lab](http://www.italianlp.it/) of [CNR-ILC](https://www.ilc.cnr.it/en/),[^msc-thesis] and then as a research scientist working on generative models for text and vision at [Aindo](https://www.aindo.com/).

[^msc-thesis]: Where I also wrote my [first paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.cmcl-1.5/) and my [master's thesis](/msc-thesis/introduction.html) on interpreting language models for linguistic complexity assessment using human eye-tracking recordings.

In 2021, I moved to the University of Groningen to start a PhD with [Arianna Bisazza](https://www.cs.rug.nl/~bisazza/), [Malvina Nissim](https://malvinanissim.github.io/) and [Grzegorz Chrupała](https://grzegorz.chrupala.me/) as part of the [GroNLP group](https://www.rug.nl/research/clcg/research/cl/?lang=en) and the [InDeep consortium](https://projects.illc.uva.nl/indeep/). My PhD project focused on [actionable interpretability for machine translation](/phd-thesis), and in 2022 I spent three nice months in NYC for an internship at Amazon Translate.

Over the course of my PhD, I became increasingly interested in building open-source tools and interfaces to democratize access to interpretability methods by improving their usability, efficiency and scalability. This led me to develop [Inseq](https://github.com/inseq-team/inseq), a library for conducting attributional analyses of LLMs, and to contribute to the development of other interpretability tools such as [NNsight](https://nnsight.net) and [Interpreto](https://github.com/FOR-sight-ai/interpreto).

After finishing my PhD in 2025, I joined the [Bau Lab](https://baulab.info/) of Northeastern University to work on scalable interpretability methods and interfaces for the [National Deep Inference Fabric (NDIF)](https://ndif.us/) team. There, I helped design the [NDIF ecosystem](https://ndif.us/tools/) and run the [Aletheia's Quest](https://aletheias-quest.github.io) deception detection competition.

## Now

*(Last updated: September 2026)*

I am now transitioning out of my postdoctoral position to serve full-time as co-founder and technical director at [Parallax](https://parallx.ai), a London-based non-profit research organisation I recently co-founded with my colleague and friend [Mario Giulianelli](https://glnmario.github.io/).

Our current work focuses on scalable methods and infrastructure for analysing the internal beliefs, plans and goals of AI agents – an area that has become increasingly important in light of recent evidence of [evaluation awareness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/eval-awareness-browsecomp), [emergent misalignment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking) and other worrying phenomena in frontier systems.
